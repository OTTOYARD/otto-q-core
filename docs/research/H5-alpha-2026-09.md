## FINDINGS

First standing-watch cycle. The frontier is split cleanly into two camps: constraint programming on
the scheduling side keeps getting cheaper and better-benchmarked (and there is a new CP engine worth
benchmarking against the kernel's current CP-SAT decide path), and the learning side is producing
strong dispatching policies that still do **not** solve the fallback problem — none of the learned
schedulers published this cycle carries a credible out-of-distribution fallback, which is exactly why
OTTO-Q's deterministic-decide-plus-cuOpt-deferral architecture is the right shape. On the energy/data
side, the single most actionable find is a real charge-curve dataset (SoC + power time series) large
enough to be a cross-platform prior, plus two mature open-source simulators the twin can wrap for
calibration. The provisional target areas stay unclaimed on the IP side (see H6); on the literature
side, "recall timing" as a first-class problem remains a gap — the nearest work treats it as a
by-product of charging-window or OTA-window scheduling.

Top three exploitable items are listed first, then the full catalog by scope area.

## FOR CLAUDE CODE

### TOP 3 EXPLOITABLE ITEMS

1. **PyJobShop (CP modeling library + 9,000-instance solver benchmark)** — L4 — *yes*
   - Leon Lan, Joost Berkhout, "PyJobShop: Solving scheduling problems with constraint programming in Python," arXiv:2502.13483 (Feb 2025). https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.13483 · https://github.com/PyJobShop/PyJobShop
   - A single Python interface over OR-Tools CP-SAT and IBM CP Optimizer with a general scheduling model (interval vars, renewable/cumulative resources, disjunctive machines). Benchmark over 9,000+ instances from machine- and project-scheduling literature: OR-Tools is competitive on job-shop/project and gives stronger lower bounds; CP Optimizer scales better on permutation and large instances (up to 48,000 tasks).
   - **Why it matters:** it is the cleanest existing abstraction for the exact problem class the kernel's decide path solves (disjunctive machines + cumulative resources). Worth adopting as a modeling layer *and* as a benchmark harness to prove CP-SAT-class performance on the canonical depot shape.

2. **Real charge-curve data (UrbanEV + DCFC charge-curve dataset) + EV2Gym / SpiceEV simulators** — L1 — *yes*
   - **UrbanEV (a.k.a. ST-EVCDP-v2):** 24,798 public charging piles, six months, 5-minute and 1-hour resolution. Li et al., *Scientific Data* 12:523 (2025). https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-025-04874-4 · https://github.com/IntelligentSystemsLab/UrbanEV
   - **DCFC charge-curve dataset:** per-session SoC **and** charging-power time series; "Deep learning predicts real-world electric vehicle direct current charging" (S. Li et al., *Nature Communications*, 2025). https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-65970-y
   - **EV2Gym:** RL/gymnasium EV smart-charging (V2G) simulator with configurable charging topology, transformer power limits, heterogeneous EV specs. https://github.com/StavrosOrf/EV2Gym
   - **SpiceEV** (RL-Institut): discrete-event EV fleet charging simulator with trip-driven charging events. https://github.com/rl-institut/spice_ev
   - **Why it matters:** these are the cheapest credible priors for the cross-platform duration/energy model — the DCFC dataset gives the raw SoC-vs-power curve shape the twin needs per asset class, and the two simulators are wrappable as calibration/benchmark harnesses (they already model a shared power feed as a hard constraint). Extends the existing ACN-Data / NYC TLC / CA DMV / NOAA registry rather than duplicating it.

3. **Multi-skilled RCPSP frameworks (skill-tiered resource pools + flexible modes)** — L4 — *yes*
   - "A solution framework for multi-skilled project scheduling problems with hierarchical skills," *Journal of Scheduling* 28:289–310 (Springer, published online 2025-03-23). https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10951-025-00836-1.pdf — MSRCPSP where resources are individuals mastering categorical skills; hierarchical skill levels.
   - "A MILP Formulation for the Pre-emptive Flexible-Mode Multi-Skilled RCPSP" (P-FM-MS-RCPSP): time-indexed MILP with preemption, multiple execution modes (alternative operation groups), multi-skilled resources. https://fount.aucegypt.edu/faculty_journal_articles/6425
   - **Why it matters:** these two formulations are the published ancestors of the exact constraints named in the kernel-gap list — skill-tiered resource pools and alternative-operation groups. They give the build track a proven constraint vocabulary (and benchmark instances) to copy for the declarative pack schema, instead of inventing one.

## OPEN QUESTIONS

1. **OptalCP vs CP-SAT on the canonical depot shape** — the MDPI benchmark only covers JSSP; nothing yet shows OptalCP's edge on *cumulative-resource + disjunctive + release-times* instances of the OTTO-Q shape. A head-to-head on a generated depot instance set is the obvious next experiment (build track).
2. **Is "recall timing" genuinely unowned, or owned under a name we haven't searched?** Scope 4 found no primary-object papers; next cycle should probe "service scheduling", "condition-based maintenance scheduling", and "opportunistic maintenance" as adjacent vocabularies before declaring the gap.
3. **Cross-operator shared-charging literature is thin** — one 2026 paper (collaborative scheduling of shared stations). Confirm whether anything on *multi-tenant* arbitration/settlement (as distinct from cost-sharing) has appeared, or whether that remains the clean L2 opening it appears to be (correlates with H6 target area (a) remaining unclaimed).
