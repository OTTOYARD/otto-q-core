# The battery planner on ten seeds, every car the same (research lab, 2026-11-06)

**Question.** Does a forward-planning battery (a linear program re-solved every five minutes and followed through the MPC
bridge, `battery_lp.py`) beat the twin's day plan (`ottoq_bess_day_plan`, 0797's settings) on the bill, and which forecast
should it plan on? The first readings (`g524_lab_2026-11-06.md`) were two and three seeds, and on three of them the cars
came out different from the base day's: the arm's registration draw was keyed on a minted id (G525). This study runs on
`ottoq_lab_0801` (the lab template with 0801, so a seed's cars reproduce), on ten seeds.

## Method

- **Days.** `normal_day`, 116 cars, 12 hours from 6 AM, seeds 171717, 424242, 515151, 616161, 717171, 818181, 919191,
  282828, 373737 and 464646. Each seed's base day is the day plan as it runs (`--tag b801`), its forecast logged every tick
  (a STABLE read that changes nothing: the 0801 base days bill exactly as the earlier base days of the same seeds).
- **Planner days**, five per seed, each the same seed with the battery planned as a linear program (the 600 kWh call
  reserve from 2 PM, the two guards), on one forecast of the depot's load before the battery:
  - `p801shape`: another day's load, step by step (seed 171717's day; 424242's for 171717 itself);
  - `p801mean`: the learned forecast's mean for the next four hours, the shape beyond;
  - `p801q80`, `p801q90`, `p801q95`: the learned mean plus the 80th, 90th or 95th percentile of its misses, per half hour
    ahead.
- **The learned forecast** (`forecast_learn.py`): a ridge regression per half hour ahead on what the day plan computes at
  the tick (the cars on a charger now, the cars coming home, the queue, the last hour's draw, the building, the sun) and
  the hour, fitted for each seed on the other nine seeds' base days only (`--exclude-seed`). Its misses are each training
  day's misses when that day was left out of the fit (`--offset-from cv`), so the band is what the forecast misses on a
  day it never saw. On the ten base days, left one seed out: mean absolute error 105-152 kW at every horizon against
  334-1,279 kW for the day plan's own rule, and the bands cover 80.2%, 90.0% and 94.9% of half hours.
  A linear quantile regression at the same levels was also graded (`forecast_upper.py`): it covers less (78.1% at 0.8)
  and is no tighter, so it was not run.
- **Bills** (`bound_energy.py`): each day pays for its own highest 30-minute average (21.40 USD/kW-month over 30 days),
  TOU energy and $0.02 a kWh of wear, given at the base day's end-of-day charge (the stored-energy difference at the day
  plan's replacement cost, $0.070 a kWh); no battery at the same charge; perfect foresight (the day's load known, ending
  no emptier than the base day), with and without the 600 kWh reserve. A planner day is compared only when every
  car-facing line of its scorecard is the base day's.
- **Choosing the quantile without the day it is graded on**: for each seed, the learned forecast with the lowest mean bill
  over the other seeds whose cars are the same in every plan, and its bill on this one (`planner_table.py`).

## What it says

1. **A forecast's mean is not enough: its band is the value.** The learned mean is 3-12 times closer to what the depot
   draws than the day plan's own forecast, and planned on it the battery is no better than on a copy of another day's
   load ($86.38 against $85.61). Planned on the mean plus the 80th percentile of the misses it has on days it never saw,
   it is the best of the six on average and on 7 of 8 seeds. The demand charge is set by the highest half hour, so the
   planner needs to know how high the next half hours could go; a mean shows it room under the billed peak that the real
   load then fills (g524 record), a band shows it the room that is really there. A wider band costs again: at the 95th
   percentile the plan saves energy for afternoon peaks that do not come and sits out the morning surge (424242: no
   discharge into the 6:15 AM surge of 1,840 kW, against 1,186 kW on the mean; its bill $1,403.51 against $1,306.06).
2. **What is left is mostly the battery's charge at 6 AM.** Where the battery starts the day with 1,900-2,100 kWh
   (171717, 616161, 373737) the 80th-percentile planner saves $196-223 and its billed half hour moves off the morning
   surge to the afternoon; where it starts with 500-800 kWh (424242, 515151, 818181) it saves $27-78 and the 6:30-8 AM
   surge still sets the bill. A 12-hour lab day starts at 6 AM with the battery as the boot left it, so it cannot show the
   one thing a planner would do before that surge: fill the battery overnight at the cheapest rate. That is the next
   measurement (whole days, the learned forecast fitted on nights as well).
3. **Production's optimizer already has this shape.** The AWS service behind the MPC bridge
   (`ottoq-intelligence/app/optimizers/energy_mpc.py`, read 2026-11-06) takes a load forecast and a per-step
   `uncertainty_kw` band for the peak. The request production builds (`ottoq_energy_mpc_replan`) sends neither a forecast
   like this one nor a band: its load is the current EV draw decayed 10% a step plus predicted arrivals, one flat
   $0.06/kWh price, no billed peak so far and no call reserve. What this study prices is the build: the learned forecast
   refit each night from the depot's own days (an estimate, which rule 10 lets production learn), its 80th-percentile miss
   as the band, the tariff and the reserve in the request, and plan-follow (0799) on.

Lab days only, 116 cars, one scenario (`normal_day`), 12 hours, each day paying for its own peak: a reading of the method
on the twin's demand model, not a forecast of production's bill.
